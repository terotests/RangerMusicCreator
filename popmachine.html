Ranger Pop Machine

A lot of instrumental pop is one short riff, repeated and changed over a loop of four chords. The 1970s synth hits, the 1980s TV themes and the stadium anthems all do it, and so did Bach: his preludes take one figure and walk it through a chain of chords. The machine builds songs that way.

The riff is a public-domain figure, one or two bars of Bach, Pachelbel, Vivaldi, Mozart, Beethoven, Grieg or Rimsky-Korsakov, or a new motif the machine writes. The chord loop comes from the CHORD LOOPS tab, where each loop says how it moves. Then the riff is worked on, and the screen tells you how:

  • RIFF HOLDS · CHORDS MOVE: the riff stays the same while the harmony changes under it.
  • SEQUENCE ↑2: the riff moves up or down the scale with the chord, keeping its shape.
  • TRANSPOSED +5: the riff moves in exact semitones, the way a rock riff does.
  • BROKEN CHORDS: the figure is re-voiced onto each new chord, as in Bach's Prelude in C.
  • ANSWER: every fourth bar the riff breaks off and lands, like a reply.
  • HALF TIME in the breakdown, DOUBLED in octaves in the chorus, and a KEY CHANGE to lift the last chorus.

The path runs from Baroque pop (1966) through Moog pop (1972), TV drive (1982), the anthem (1986) and Eurodance (1994) to EDM pop (2015). Only the habits of those styles are used: no melody, recording or arrangement of any record. The famous synth hits and film themes are still protected, so none of their tunes are here.

MODERN turns it round: the riff is in the bass. A deep sub bass plays long notes that walk high, middle and low and then bend up a little, pumping under a kick on every beat and a clap on two and four. A simple 16-bar string theme and quiet horns sit well below it, and the drum machine fills in only at the end of a section. In the breakdown the bass and kick drop out, the theme plays alone and ends on a drum hit, and the bass comes back. The themes are written by the machine. KEY CHANGE! jumps to the second drop.

The sounds are all synthesized in your browser. What you save is yours to use, in games too, with no royalties.
